# Case studies: first candidate vs. published title

Six real episodes (July–Aug 2026) where the skill's first candidate differed from the
human-published title. Read these before generating — the goal is for candidate #1
(the publish pick) to land on the title Liron/Ori would actually publish.

Transcripts + ground truth for all six live in
`~/Desktop/ClaudeCode/transcripts/doom-titles-episodes/` (see its README.md).

---

## 1. UC Berkeley street interviews (03FKH3UxgTg)

- **First candidate:** `I Asked UC Berkeley Students: Will AI Kill Everyone!?`
- **Published:** `Debating College Students About AI Extinction`

Lessons:
- First-person stunt framing ("I Asked…") lost here to the channel's verb
  **Debating** — plain participial framing that says what the episode is. (Not an
  absolute ban: first person can work when the personal stunt is itself the story.)
- Broaden the group: "College Students" travels further than "UC Berkeley Students".
- "AI Extinction" is the broad-audience stakes phrase; "Will AI Kill Everyone!?" with
  interrobang reads as stunt clickbait and got cut.

## 2. Doom Train @ Manifest 2026 (jE1nZVjyb5A)

- **First candidate:** `Doom Debates Live @ Manifest 2026 — Producer Ori vs. Everyone`
- **Published:** `Where Do YOU Get Off the Doom Train? Live Debate at Manifest 2026`

Lessons:
- Lead with the content hook, put the event/format label second — never lead with the
  channel name ("Doom Debates Live @…" is redundant on the channel's own page).
- Use franchise language the audience knows: **the Doom Train**.
- Direct-address **YOU** in caps is a house move.
- Don't center internal personalities ("Producer Ori") that a browsing viewer doesn't know.

## 3. Seb Krier / PauseAI gun meme (J3l7Yt8-af0)

- **First candidate:** `Did Google DeepMind's Policy Lead Just Threaten PauseAI?`
- **Published:** `Special Report: Google DeepMind Frontier AI Policy Lead's Controversial SH*TPOST`

Lessons:
- **Never overclaim.** "Threaten" accuses beyond what the episode defends; the
  published title describes accurately ("Controversial SH*TPOST") and gets its spice
  from the censored word instead.
- Use the **exact job title**: "Frontier AI Policy Lead", not "Policy Lead".
- Drop small orgs with no name recognition (PauseAI).
- "Special Report:" is a real format label on this channel — use it for commentary on
  a news moment.

## 4. State of the Show 2026 (JCEIjy7-N-w)

- **First candidate:** `Doom Debates State of the Show 2026 — Our Mission, Money & What's NEXT`
- **Published:** `Doom Debates State of the Show 2026: What's Working, What's Next, and Why We Need YOU`

Lessons:
- Keep the format label; fix the list. The published list is a **parallel triplet**
  ending in a viewer-facing appeal: "…and Why We Need YOU" (direct-address YOU again).
- "Money" and gratuitous caps ("NEXT") got cut; the appeal to the viewer is the hook.

## 5. Livestream 7/31 (EK-qIcdgIVw)

- **First candidate:** `The AI Escaped Its Box — And Anthropic Believed Its Excuse`
- **Published:** `AI Is Becoming A VIRUS, Leopold's Fund CRASH, Gary Marcus's Predictions Didn't Age Well`

Lessons:
- A multi-topic news livestream NEVER gets a single-story title. It gets a
  **comma-separated headline triptych**: one clause per story, named people
  (Leopold, Gary Marcus), at most one caps word per clause (VIRUS, CRASH).
- No date or "DD Live News" branding in the title.

## 6. Alex Turner / DeepMind resignation (pGlJQHVeKIA)

- **First candidate:** `Ex-DeepMind Safety Researcher: "Google Broke Its Founding Promise" — Alex Turner`
- **Published:** `Google DeepMind AI Safety Researcher Who Just RESIGNED Says P(Doom) is 25% — Debate with Alex Turner`

Lessons:
- Full canonical org name up front: "Google DeepMind", never "Ex-DeepMind" — search
  and recognition value wins.
- The news verb in caps + "Just" recency: **Just RESIGNED**.
- A **concrete number beats a quote-pull**: "P(Doom) is 25%" replaced
  "Google Broke Its Founding Promise".
- Guest debates end with the episode-type tag: `— Debate with [Name]`.
- Published length = 100 chars. Long is fine when every word earns its place.

## 7. Hugging Face hack livestream (RczYubQzXbI)

- **First candidates:** `OpenAI's AIs Secretly Plotted for MONTHS Before Hacking Hugging Face — DD Live News 8/7` ·
  `OpenAI's AI SECRETLY Hacked Hugging Face While Its Engineers Slept`
- **Published:** `OpenAI's Model Just ATTACKED Them — Terrifying Security Incident Should Be A Loud Warning Shot`

Lessons:
- Compress the incident to one shock clause with a caps news verb + "Just":
  "OpenAI's Model Just ATTACKED Them". Narrative detail ("Plotted for MONTHS",
  "While Its Engineers Slept") got cut.
- **Second clause = Liron's editorial take on why it matters** ("…Should Be A Loud
  Warning Shot"), not more plot.
- No livestream/date branding ("DD Live News 8/7").

## 8. Eli Goldfine, 14-year-old forecaster (Bqk5vqpzG_o)

- **First candidate:** `This 14-Year-Old Prodigy Rides the AI Doom Train to the END — Debate with Eli Goldfine`
- **Published:** `This 14-Year-Old Thinks My P(Doom) Is Too High — Eli Goldfine`

Lessons:
- **Personal-stakes clash beats franchise metaphor.** The published hook is the guest
  challenging Liron directly ("Thinks My P(Doom) Is Too High") — first-person "My" as
  *stakes* is a house move, distinct from the banned first-person *stunt* framing
  ("I Asked…"). The Doom Train ride metaphor lost.
- Cut hype nouns: "Prodigy" got dropped; the bare age carries the surprise.
- The guest tag can be plain `— [Name]` — "Debate with" isn't mandatory when the
  title already states the disagreement.
- P(Doom) jargon is fine when it IS the conflict (the guest's whole thesis was
  Liron's number being too high).
- No caps word at all in the published title — emphasis is optional, not required.

## 9. Michael Vassar, former Singularity Institute president (4GbFng6htaQ)

- **First candidate:** `Former MIRI President Says Doomers Like Me Are DOOMING the World — Michael Vassar`
- **Session pick (brainstormed with Ori):** `"Bad People" — Why This Yudkowsky Insider Soured on the Rationalists & PauseAI — Michael Vassar`
- **Published:** `Former Singularity Institute President: Rationalists Are DOOMING the World`

Lessons:
- **Name the org as it was called when the guest held the role.** Vassar led the
  *Singularity Institute*; "Former MIRI President" is anachronistic. Canonical-name
  accuracy is period-accurate, not just present-day.
- **`[Former Role at Org]: [flat declaration of their thesis]`** — a colon-led
  authority credential followed by the bomb. Cleanest structure yet for
  contrarian-insider episodes; it beat both the first instinct and the session pick.
- **A strong role descriptor can replace the guest tag entirely.** No `— Michael
  Vassar`, no `— Debate with`. When the name has less recognition value than the
  title, the title IS the identification.
- **The provocative named group beats personal stakes here.** "Rationalists Are
  DOOMING the World" outranked "Doomers Like Me" — case study #8's personal-stakes
  move doesn't generalize when there's a bigger, more recognizable target to name.
- Quote-hook framing ("Bad People", "Soured on…") lost to the flat declaration.

## 10. Madvertising 2023 archival crosspost (M6gNwZWY-T8)

- **First candidate:** `AI Will BRICK the Universe — Liron on Adam Singer's Madvertising Podcast (2023)`
- **Session picks:** `Liron Predicted It 3 YEARS Ago! "GPT Is About to Be the Strongest Hacker the World Has Ever Known"` ·
  `Liron Predicted GPT Hacking 3 YEARS Ago! (Madvertising, 2023)`
- **Published:** `What I Said About AI Hackers in 2023 Is Now Uncomfortable to Watch`

Lessons:
- **Archival crossposts are titled by how the old claim reads TODAY** — a first-person
  retrospective ("What I Said… Is Now Uncomfortable to Watch"), not the boldest old
  quote. The hook is the prediction aging into reality, framed from the present.
- **Understated dread beat triumphalism.** Both session picks bragged ("Predicted It
  3 YEARS Ago!"); the published title sells unease instead. The channel's register is
  ominous, not self-congratulatory.
- **Host-show branding got dropped entirely** ("Adam Singer's Madvertising Podcast",
  the "(2023)" parenthetical) — an external show with no recognition value earns no
  title space. Extends case #3's drop-small-orgs rule to podcasts/hosts.
- No caps word, no quote marks — a plain first-person sentence carries it.
